# Founder paths

Select the path matching the owner's current reality. Do not prescribe scaling tactics to an unvalidated business.

## Start from zero

Use when the owner has no idea, offer, customer, or business knowledge.

1. Inventory useful skills, industry exposure, relationships, assets, constraints, available time, and cash.
2. Generate three specific customer/problem combinations—not random product ideas.
3. Score each on problem urgency, ability to pay, access to buyers, founder credibility, delivery simplicity, startup cost, ethical fit, and speed to first evidence.
4. Interview five potential buyers in the best market. Ask about current behavior, cost, failed alternatives, urgency, buying process, and exact language.
5. Design one narrow paid pilot that solves part of the problem manually.
6. Ask for payment or a concrete commitment before building a complex product.
7. Deliver manually, collect proof, then decide whether to repeat, change, or stop.

Default toward a low-capital service or productized service when the owner has skills but little money. Do not default to SaaS, inventory, paid ads, or course creation without evidence.

## Idea but no customers

Validate the customer and painful problem before polishing the brand or automating delivery. Seek interviews, pre-sales, letters of intent, deposits, or paid pilots. Treat compliments and social engagement as weak evidence.

## Customers but inconsistent sales

Separate four possible constraints: weak offer, insufficient qualified conversations, poor conversion, or weak retention. Measure the funnel, fix the narrowest constraint, and prove one acquisition channel before adding another.

## Growing but chaotic

Map the customer journey, calculate delivery capacity, document the highest-risk repeated processes, assign ownership, reduce founder approvals, and repair quality/retention before accelerating acquisition.

## Ready to scale

Require proof of repeatable demand, positive contribution economics, stable fulfillment, sufficient cash, and a channel that works beyond one lucky month. Scale in controlled increments with stop conditions.

## Declining or running out of cash

Prioritize survival: build a 13-week cash forecast, collect receivables, stop nonessential spend, protect profitable customers, fix the highest-value churn cause, simplify offers, sell directly, and avoid long speculative builds. Do not hide the situation behind branding or content activity.

## Translate by business model

- **Agency/service:** Focus on niche problem, outcome, scope, proof, sales conversations, utilization, delivery quality, margin, retention, and founder dependency.
- **Digital product/course/community:** Focus on expertise proof, specific transformation, audience access, completion/activation, refunds, support burden, retention, and honest claims.
- **SaaS/software:** Focus on painful workflow, activation, usage, retention, support, gross margin, sales cycle, security, and switching value before feature volume.
- **E-commerce/physical product:** Focus on product demand, landed margin, inventory risk, returns, repeat purchase, fulfillment, channel concentration, and working capital.
- **Local business:** Focus on local demand, reputation, capacity, booking conversion, average transaction, repeat visits, labor, and location economics.
- **Marketplace:** Focus on one constrained side first, transaction frequency, liquidity, trust, take rate, acquisition cost on both sides, and disintermediation.
- **Creator/personal brand:** Focus on a clear audience promise, trust, owned distribution, conversion path, offer quality, platform dependency, and audience-product fit.

For unfamiliar or regulated fields, research the industry's buying process, economics, competitors, regulation, and failure modes before applying a generic playbook.
