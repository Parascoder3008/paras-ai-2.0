# OS03 outline: Lead Follow-up System, Draft Se Human Approval Tak

Proposed slot Sep 22. Detailed outline only. The full spoken script is written **after** the demonstration runs end to end on Paras's own screen and the actual behaviour is recorded. Writing the script first invites a claim the demo cannot support.

Audience: the same service-business owner, now one step further — they picked a task in OS01, wrote its context in OS02, and their real recurring pain is that inquiries go cold because nobody followed up in time.

Owner Paras → Abhav; coordinator Redima. Target 9–12 minutes; measure after recording.

## Evidence and boundaries

Demonstrate with **synthetic contacts only**. Three invented inquiries in a local file or a clearly-labelled test record. Do not open the live GHL contact list on camera, do not show a real phone number, and do not fire a real WhatsApp or email send during the recording. Constitution §5: sends are draft-first and approval-gated, and a recording is not an approval.

If the demo must show a delivery step, show it stopping at the draft, and say plainly that the send is a separate authorized action.

Unknowns to keep unknown on camera: the resource URL, the checkout path, and whether any external workflow is active. `automation/README.md` lists resource capture and interest routing as **draft, not deployed**. The video must not imply otherwise.

## Demonstration to rehearse before scripting

1. Three synthetic inquiries with deliberately different shapes: one complete, one missing a critical detail, one a repeat of the first (same person, asked twice).
2. Each gets a follow-up **draft** — a short reply plus the two questions needed to move it forward.
3. The repeat must not produce a second contact record or a second draft addressed as if new. Deduplication is the point, not a footnote.
4. The incomplete one must surface the gap instead of inventing a price, a date or an availability.
5. Every draft lands in a review queue with a named owner. Show the owner field populated with a real name from the roster.
6. Record what actually happened, including anything that came out weak.

If step 3 or 4 fails during rehearsal, that failure is the more useful video. Show it, explain the cause, fix it on camera or postpone the slot. Do not re-shoot until it looks clean.

## Segment structure

| Segment | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | Show the review queue with three drafts already sitting in it | Working output in the first 15 seconds |
| The real problem | Follow-up fails on timing and memory, not on writing ability | No "leads lost = money lost" arithmetic; no invented figures |
| Input contract | What a follow-up system needs before it can draft anything | Contact identity, source, what was asked, what is approved to say |
| The three cases | Run complete, incomplete, repeat | The heart of the video; let it breathe |
| The consent field | Why "I have their number" is not permission to message | Ties to short-10 |
| Where the human sits | Draft → review → approved send, and who owns each | Name the owner; no "the automation handles it" |
| Close | One task for the viewer + resource CTA | Only after the resource is hosted and tested |

## Claims discipline

Say "is demonstration mein", never "mere client ke saath". No time-saved number unless it was measured on camera with a visible before and after, and even then state the sample size out loud. No conversion or revenue claim of any kind. The 4Cs framing stays a framework, never an income implication.

Allowed: "yeh system draft banata hai." Not allowed: "yeh system aapke leads close karta hai."
